3 framed color photographs, 107 x 100 cm each


Roddy The Pict (I don’t have tattoos but if I did I’d probably look like this)  is a personal self-portrait with a strong family dimension. The artist’s naked body, divided into three parts, is covered with the temporary tattoos that his three young sons often use. But as is always the case with this artist, the piece offers multiple levels of interpretation. An image of nudity and concealment. A vertical triptych of a body revealing itself, a sort of primitive Ecce homo . Yet another double reference: one to the intimacy of his family—that fatherly concern for the symbols his children use—and the other to the British penchant for tattoos, which, as one might have noticed, does not carry the same roguish air among them as it does here. But there’s more: a deliberate allusion to those warriors of the Scottish lowlands, the Picts who harassed Roman troops beyond Hadrian’s Wall. Their very name refers to their appearance as “painted men”—war paint, but paint nonetheless. This is why, in the title, Roderick Buchanan gives himself the nickname “Roddy the Pict,” confirming, if confirmation were needed, the profoundly pictorial nature of his entire body of work.
Jean-Marc Huitorel, exhibition curator « Roderick Buchanan : From a city of one million », 2007


Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

Roderick Buchanan

body: >-


68 color photographs mounted on Dibond, 41 x 40 cm each


Scottish Cakes is a collective self-portrait created through a photographic inventory of Scottish cakes that the artist purchased from bakeries in the working-class neighborhood of Glasgow where he lives. Photographed in a minimalist style and neatly arranged in four rows spanning two vast wall sections at La Criée, the photographs of cakes are a feast for the eyes. The work functions less as a decorative showcase of pastry-making allure and more as a quasi-clinical, conceptual installation. But paradoxically, this rigorous arrangement allows the cakes’ uniqueness and their rich contradictions to come to the fore: a profusion of colors or, conversely, strict monochromaticity; expressive, dripping textures or a restrained mastery of the cake batter; the addition of figurative motifs or the simple application of abstract elements. A game unfolds with the visitor, who then tries to guess while defusing questions about the anthropomorphic “identity” and “expressiveness” of the pastries.

Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

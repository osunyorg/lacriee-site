17 black-and-white photographs, printed on baryta paper, mounted on aluminum, 60 x 90 cm 8 black-and-white photographs, printed on baryta paper, mounted on aluminum, 40 x 60 cm 6 black-and-white photographs, printed on RC paper, mounted on aluminum, 60 x 150 cm 4 inkjet prints on fabric suspended in the exhibition space, 130 x 200 cm


During the summer of 1999, Georges Dussaud spent time in the Kerala region of India to photograph and document daily life and traditional arts. He offers a poetic perspective on India by photographing Kathakali schools—the sensual movement of the body, the satiny texture of the skin; Kalarippayatt training sessions—the tension of the fight, respect for the master; and landscapes—the rice harvest, the cattle market, and the flight of crows.  The unobtrusive and quiet equipment he uses allows him to take an intimate and empathetic approach. Dans la lumière du Kerala  is a series of black-and-white photographs and a temporary installation consisting of four inkjet prints on fabric suspended in space. Georges Dussaud’s photography is rooted in his sensibility. It is not about depicting reality, but about interpreting it and working from intuition. Black-and-white photography, natural light, and the use of high-speed film highlight an aesthetic and visual dimension, while undermining the discipline’s rigorous nature. The moment is well worth capturing. “The moment of photography is, first and foremost, that intimate instant when the photographer approaches and connects with his subject.” Gaining access to Kathakali schools to photograph the masters and the children is no easy task in these places where teaching remains traditional. Georges Dussaud knows how to forge that bond of humanity, intimacy, and respect which, combined with patience, inspires the trust of those who will pose for him.” Offering, self-surrender—this timeless moment is one of silence. It is difficult to gauge; it may seem very long but in reality be very brief. It is that moment when each person finds themselves face to face with themselves, as if standing before a mental mirror. A moment of unease until the silence makes sense. Then comes the fleeting, invisible instant—a second frozen on photographic paper. The gesture has already vanished, and yet it will remain suspended, consigned to eternity and to a multitude of gazes.” Alexandra Gillet, «Dans la lumière du Kerala : une histoire de temps»,  La Griffe , n°96, 25 octobre – 7 novembre 2000

Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

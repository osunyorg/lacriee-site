a permeable structure more than 50 meters long particleboard planks made from collected furniture


Sentier Battu was created as part of Simon Augade’s residency at the Antoine de Saint-Exupéry Group’s agricultural and horticultural high school. The work takes the form of a vast, enclosed corridor winding through the paths of the landscaped park, constructed from particleboard planks salvaged from furniture. These elements, which once defined the living space, are nailed together, creating a work of art that visitors can explore, walk through, or go around. With the active participation of high school apprentices and students in the BTS Landscape Architecture program, used furniture collected from the Emmaus community became the raw material for the creation of a structure more than fifty meters long. Wood—which is ubiquitous in parks—is treated here as an industrial, construction material that defines the space and guides the flow of visitors. Sentier Battu physically creates a break between the prescribed path and the surrounding space. The installation redefines the park by dividing it into different sections, thereby fragmenting the perception of the whole. Upon entering the corridor, the viewer steps out of the landscape’s frame and into another constructed, organized, and structured space. From the outside, the view of the landscape is obstructed. The rigid, straight, and angular lines contrast with those found in nature, creating a sense of balance with the treetops. The work thus establishes new rules, challenges conventions, and encourages visitors to move around in order to perceive and grasp it as a whole. Visitors can choose whether or not to follow the marked path, take the corridors, or go around them—at the risk of venturing off the beaten path…
Partners : -- Communauté d’Emmaüs, Hédé -- lycée agricole et horticole Groupe Antoine de Saint-Exupéry, Rennes

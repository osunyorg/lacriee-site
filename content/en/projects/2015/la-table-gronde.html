joists, fasteners, total diameter of the table: 12.54 m


Yves Chaudouët’s La Table gronde is so large—40 meters in circumference—that it cannot fit entirely within the art center’s space. The central section of the table is installed at La Criée. The other two sections are located, like satellites, at *Au bout du plongeoir* in Thorigné-Fouillard and at the Théâtre de Poche in Hédé. The work draws inspiration from the original utopian ideal of the round table, which was to enable people to gather together while setting aside all notions of precedence—the “risk” of a chairperson. First appearing in French literature in the 12th century* and made famous by the poet Chrétien de Troyes, it has become a figure that is at once literary, political, and utopian—a symbol of equality and fraternity. According to some versions of the Arthurian legend, it could seat anywhere from twelve to 1,600 knights. The sculpture, divided into three parts, extends this concept to other creative spaces. Whether its pieces are in the city or in the countryside, La Table gronde is an invitation to sit down, to settle in (take a break?): to chat, flip through pages, create, contemplate, or listen, and so on. Its stage is more than just a nod to live performance: occasionally transforming into a nomadic stage, it can actually host actors, speakers, and musicians. From this new layout and configuration emerge unexpected encounters and uses: the charming Théâtre de Poche collective and the “Autour de la table” project with “Au Bout du plongeoir” perform at La Criée, as does Yves Chaudouët’s theater company, Morphologie des Éléments. After the exhibition, the three sections of the table were reassembled at Au bout du plongeoir in Thorigné-Fouillard.




* According to some authors, the Round Table was created by a carpenter from Cornwall, southwest of Brittany, at Merlin's request, to bring together the knights of the Kingdom. Advice : -- Virginie Bourgeacq -- Serge Goacolou

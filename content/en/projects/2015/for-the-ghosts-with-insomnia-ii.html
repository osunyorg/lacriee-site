câble, ampoule, bougie, plaque de métal, dimensions variables


The absence of the lights that were once contained within the metal plates contrasts with the presence of the light bulb suspended by a wire in the installation For the Ghosts with Insomnia (II) . The work juxtaposes two light sources: one artificial, positioned above a flame emanating from a candle placed on a metal square. The installation contrasts two concepts of time—the permanence of electricity and the fragility of the burning wick. The installation can be interpreted as a metaphor for the “enlightened” modern world that emerged during the Age of Enlightenment—a time of great exploration and the advancement of science and reason—in contrast to obscurantism. For Runo Lagomarsino, the light in the works embodies modernity while revealing its hidden side—namely, the “coloniality” that accompanied its development, that is, a Eurocentric, dominant, and universalist conception of the modern world.

Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

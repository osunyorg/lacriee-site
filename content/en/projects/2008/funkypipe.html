Alexandre Perigot

body: >-


steel tubes and panels, electric motors, wallpaper; dimensions of steel panels: 210 x 62 cm; dimensions vary depending on the installation in the space


A famous anonymous folk painting from Baku, Azerbaijan, is printed on wallpaper to serve as decor… much like those sunset tapestries that once adorned the walls of many private homes. With the painting reimagined as wallpaper by Alexandre Perigot, the sunset is still there, but its romanticism is pushed to the limit by the presence in the foreground of a forest of oil fields in Baku… There is a disconcerting sincerity in this popular image, as well as an indecency toward art and the consumption of raw materials… This folk painting isn’t so bad after all… It’s actually very beautiful… and semantically very effective. In the exhibition space, the Baku tapestry painting is complemented by three sculptures of shelves made of steel tubing and steel trays.  These shelves are powered by an electric motor that causes the steel tubes to sway in a lascivious, hip-swinging motion reminiscent of belly dancing. On each shelf, fragments cut from steel cylinders lie neatly arranged. As the shelves sway, the sections of tubing try in vain to join together in a bid to create vertical power… But… No way… Funkypipe is too refined a work to raise the plunger and squeeze out the precious liquid. Funkypipe operates at the intersection of art, geopolitics, and economic history, foreshadowing the end of oil and, in a jubilant blend of erotic imagery, visual artistry, and choreography, burying both the precious “black gold” and the era of the beautiful aesthetic image. Larys Frogier, Alexandre Perigot :Palaispopeye ,  Museu Coleção Berardo, Lisbonne ;  La Criée centre d’art contemporain, Rennes ; Cneai, Chatou, 2008

Collection : -- Museu Colecção Berardo, Lisbonne -- courtesy galerie Suzanne Tarasiève, Paris
Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

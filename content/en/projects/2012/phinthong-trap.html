Pratchaya Phinthong

body: >-


prototype and limited edition of 500 pieces blue fabric, 120 x 120 x 120 cm



















In the small room, Pratchaya Phinthong presents a prototype trap designed in collaboration with researchers and scientists from the biology department at Lusaka University in Zambia. It resembles a small blue tent that is easy to set up and transport. This experimental trap is presented as an eco-friendly and sustainable alternative to nuclear biology. It also gives researchers the opportunity to capture flies in order to study the migration and evolution of different species.  In addition, Pratchaya Phinthong commissioned a Thai company that specializes in manufacturing meditation tents for monasteries to produce 500 traps. Alongside the exhibition, the 500 blue tents are being deployed in Zambia, Ethiopia, and Tanzania to test their effectiveness in the field. These pieces are contained in small bags bearing the name of La Criée, the Rennes Center for Contemporary Art and Biennial, as well as the title of the exhibition, sleeping sickness . In return, Pratchaya Phinthong invited scientists and biologists to send him photographs of the traps in their natural settings, using whatever means were available to them. Among the first documents received was a report sent by the Department of Biology at Lusaka University in Zambia detailing the effectiveness of this new trap—T1 refers to the official, approved prototype; T2 to the prototype on display at La Criée, which researchers have renamed the Phinthong trap  ; and T3 to the trap designed by Pratchaya Phinthong and improved by the researchers. Additional documents and photographs will be added gradually to the La Criée section, documenting the work’s evolution across time and space.


















Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

Pratchaya Phinthong, vue de l'exposition sleeping sickness , La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes, 2012 © Benoît Mauras

body: >-


polystyrene, acrylic resin, paint, electromagnet



After giving us a glimpse of the set for the upcoming stage adaptation of Ray Bradbury’s science-fiction short story, The Savannah , Virginie Yassef now reveals the sculptures of her first characters. The play tells the futuristic story of the Hadley family, who live in a high-tech house. The parents, Georges and Lydia, envision a playroom for their children where they can virtually project all their wishes. This projection system goes awry when the children choose an African bush with lifelike lions as their backdrop… The fictional parents are represented here by two large Galápagos tortoise legs. For the artist, these legs and the parents share the trait of having claws that they lose when they are afraid. Equipped with an electromagnet system, the claws drop at regular intervals. In Virginie Yassef’s world, the strange—or even the supernatural—always emerges where you least expect it.


* The Veldt  ( La Savane ) is part of the short story collection  L’homme illustré, publié pour la première fois aux États-Unis en 1951 et en France en 1954
Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

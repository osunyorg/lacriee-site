plâtre, bois, métal, soie, coton, laine et feutre
285 x 106 x 470 cm





Ami(e)  is a slightly distorted replica of a small, impromptu sculpture I created a few months before the exhibition. This piece represents a transition to another dimension—that of dreams and “potential space,” as the child psychoanalyst Donald Winnicott* would say. Ami(e)  is not entirely a fictional character, nor is it entirely a rock. From its iron rods unfolds a long braid woven from various pieces of silk, wool, and felt, which extends into the middle of the room. Ami(e)  is a sculpture: that is to say, a bit of both, combining the whimsy of one with the seriousness of the other, without any established genre or category. Ami(e)  could have as a friend a sculpture by Franz West, Phyllida Barlow, or Noguchi, as well as a rock from the coast of Brittany or from Joshua Tree National Park in California.









* Donald Winnicott, Jeu et réalité , 1971 (trad. éditions Gallimard, Folio/essais, 1975)
Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

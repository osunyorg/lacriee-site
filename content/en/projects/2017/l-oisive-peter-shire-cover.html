Félicia Atkinson

body: >-


table, stools, and sculptures painted wood, wood, raw clay, pebbles, hemp twine, felt, wool, balsa wood, pigment, oil chalk, modeling clay, wire 100 x 100 x 67 cm








« Idle Hands are the devil’s playground » as the saying goes: Idle hands are the devil’s playground. Here, the goal is to turn that saying on its head and sing the praises of idleness and piracy. I conceived a table that was part bootleg (pirated version) and part “cover song” (cover version) of two tables by Los Angeles-based designer and artist Peter Shire, on which I arranged a number of impromptu sculptures made of raw clay and painted felt, inviting the public to engage in a game with no specific rules or conventions. This “mixed” table, “sampled” table and the objects covering it offer visitors the chance to get bored together, to spend time with no particular reason or purpose—a bit like when you’re online listening to covers of well-known songs or medleys on YouTube, or playing a game on your cell phone—but here in a completely analog way and without instructions, through wood, clay, and fabric.










Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

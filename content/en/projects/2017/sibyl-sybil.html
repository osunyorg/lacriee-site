This video, produced in 2017, consists of a series of fragmented shots, accompanied by a recording of actress Laure-Lucille Simon reading an excerpt from one of American poet Wallace Stevens’s final poems, titled The Sail of Ulysses *. “This is a passage that stood out to me in particular when I read this poem, as often happens when one reads.”** The text is read alternately in French and English by the actress: -- What form does the sibyl take? She is the sibyl of the self, The self as sibyl, whose diamond, Whose highest embrace of all riches is poverty, whose jewel found at the very center of the earth is need. To this end, the form of the sibyl Is a blind thing groping in the dark, searching for its shape, A shape that is frail—a hand, a back, A dream too meager, too destitute To be remembered—this old, worn-out form Bent toward nothingness, A woman gazing into the distance along the road, A child dozing in the midst of her own life.


* Wallace Steven, Opus Posthumous : poems, plays, prose , New York, A.A. Knopf, 1957. Traduit par Julien Bismuth et Laure-Lucille Simon.
** Julien Bismuth, correspondance du 13 juin 2017
Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

This video, produced in 2017, consists of a series of fragmented shots, accompanied by a recording of actress Laure-Lucille Simon reading an excerpt from one of American poet Wallace Stevens’s final poems, titled The Sail of Ulysses*.

“This is a passage that stood out to me in particular when I read this poem, as often happens when one reads.”** The text is read alternately in French and English by the actress:

-- What form does the sibyl take?
She is the sibyl of the self,
The self as sibyl, whose diamond,
Whose highest embrace of all riches
is poverty, whose jewel found
at the very center of the earth
is need. To this end, the form of the sibyl
Is a blind thing groping in the dark, searching for its shape,
A shape that is frail—a hand, a back,
A dream too meager, too destitute
To be remembered—this old, worn-out form
Bent toward nothingness,
A woman gazing into the distance along the road,
A child dozing in the midst of her own life.

* Wallace Steven, Opus Posthumous : poems, plays, prose, New York, A.A. Knopf, 1957. Traduit par Julien Bismuth et Laure-Lucille Simon.
** Julien Bismuth, correspondance du 13 juin 2017

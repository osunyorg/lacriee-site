mural, poster, and lighting, 500 x 300 cm


In Virginie Yassef’s world, the strange—and even the supernatural—always emerges where we least expect it. Through the objects she repurposes and to which she applies variations in scale or texture, she challenges our geographical, spatial, and temporal reference points to create narratives that are both funny and cruel. Her work is rooted in the concept of movement—both her own travels and the experience she offers viewers of her installations. At La Criée, she presents La Savane , an installation combining painting, posters, and light that functions as a full-fledged set inspired by Ray Bradbury’s science-fiction short story The Veldt (La Savane) *. The story tells the futuristic tale of the Hadley family, who live in a high-tech house. Georges and Lydia, the parents, come up with the idea of a playroom for their children where they can virtually project all their wishes. This projection system goes awry when the children choose an African bush with lifelike lions as their setting…


* The Veldt  ( La Savane ) is part of the short story collection   L’homme illustré, publié pour la première fois aux États-Unis en 1951 et en France en 1954

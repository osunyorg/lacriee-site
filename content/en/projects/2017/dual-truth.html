Yann Sérandour

body: >-


sound installation, 2 vinyl records, wooden display stand, 2 Dual turntables, 2-channel mixing console, tube amplifier, pairs of speakers, display case, 265 x 305 x 50 cm, edition of 1




In the listening room’s display case, two Dual turntables play Vérité du clavecin  including Chambonnières’ Suite in D minor simultaneously, on demand. The album covers, from the 1977 release, are stacked on top of one another on a wooden display stand. The sound is played through speakers via a high-fidelity (Hi-Fi) tube amplifier, a system designed to reproduce the recorded source as faithfully as possible. The time lag between the two sources, which gradually widens, contrasts the two “truths” stated on the album cover. The cover does not mention any composer’s name, prioritizing performance and reproduction over the original author. In this sound installation titled Dual Truth , Yann Sérandour creates a counterpoint by layering two musical lines and displaying a sound-reproduction device—intended to be transparent—in a glass case. In this way, he highlights not only the reproduction of sound through the evolution of recording and broadcasting systems, but also the disconnect in its reception. The harpsichord piece, originally performed and danced to in the salons of the European aristocracy, is now reproduced and displayed as a museum piece—an object of collection and fetishization.

Farid Redouani

body: >-


installation, wood, cement, paint, graffiti, mattress, aluminum 300 x 300 cm


In 2000, Farid Redouani created a replica of a prison cell in Algiers as part of the “ Nights of Correspondence ” event organized by the French Cultural Center. Visitors were able to enter this enclosed space, write a letter there, which was then placed in a mailbox and sent to its recipient by mail. The cell, as conceived by Farid Redouani, resonates with his pictorial work, in which bodies and faces are confined to a minimal space for survival. The cell, therefore, does not merely document the reality of prison. It is more of a metaphor for any space of confinement or withdrawal, referring as much to a mental space as to the precarious living conditions endured by Algiers’ working class. This understanding of the cell as a generic space of confinement ties in with Khaled Belaïd’s series of photographic triptychs. Indeed, his photographs depict the deadly flood of November 10, 2001, which devastated the working-class neighborhood of Bab-El-Oued, where the photographer lives. More than 1,500 residents lost their lives, buried under tons of mud and debris. The photographs capture both the devastation of the neighborhood and the spirit of mutual aid and solidarity among its residents. These same residents had criticized the government’s inaction in the face of the dilapidated condition of the homes and the failure to bring the plumbing up to safety standards.

Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

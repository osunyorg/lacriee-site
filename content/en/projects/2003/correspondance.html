slides and video clips transferred to DVD, sound, 26 min


Part 1, 13 min By alternating between photographs and short video clips, Kader Attia illustrates the connections and differences between the lifestyles of some of his family members living in the Paris suburbs (Garges-les-Gonesses) and other relatives who have remained in the Algerian Atlas region. The entire projection is accompanied by a soundtrack that takes viewers on a journey through various FM radio stations, broadcasting news and popular Franco-Algerian music. Straddling the line between ethnological observation and a sensitive portrayal of human beings, this film, Correspondance , offers a subtle and moving exploration of issues related to the socioeconomic realities of uprooting, the search for ancestral values, identity, and cultural alienation. Part 2, 13 min, subtitled The Girl Who Wanted to Get Married : This second part presents a filmed portrait of Samira, Kader Attia’s cousin. This sequence actually depicts Samira’s proposal to the artist’s brother. Sensitive and subtle, the portrait is presented here as a delicate game of seduction that oscillates between the young woman’s self-description, the narrative of her life values, and the assertion of a physical—even sensual—presence before the camera. Kader Attia thus becomes not merely a go-between in marriage negotiations, but the recipient and messenger of a surprisingly vivid narrative. What is most moving about this work is, first and foremost, a gesture of offering to his loved ones, who are separated between France and Algeria. This gift of image and sound to others runs through the slideshow from start to finish, as if the aim were to reconcile two worlds that are so different and yet so close at the same time. The visual impact of this correspondence lies in the palpable tension between what is happening on the sidelines and between the images: the memory of places the artist rediscovers in Algeria, the beauty of simple moments shared in Garges-les-Gonesses or Sétif, and the tribute to an Algerian culture that the children of immigrants find very difficult to recognize. The *punctum* Roland Barthes speaks of is thus revealed through visual details that fill the image: the gentle swaying of the flowers among his family’s graves, the uneven texture of a wall bearing the graffiti  “Visa,” the texture of the skin on a hand holding out a photograph, the empty space separating the apartment building from the housing project, and the blurred face of a young man in the corner of the image. All of these sensitive points punctuate the slideshow and form the thread that ties the images together. The aim, therefore, is not to document according to a narrative logic, but rather to create qualitative leaps of the punctum from one image to the next. Larys Frogier, « Photographier le lien, vivre la
mémoire », Algerian Perspectives, Vibrant Creations, Exhibition Catalog, La Criée, centre d’art contemporain, Rennes, 2003
* « The punctum is a kind of subtle off-screen element, as if the image
were directing desire beyond what it shows: not only toward "the rest"
of nudity, not only toward the fantasy of a sexual act, but toward the
absolute perfection of a being, soul and body intertwined. » Roland
Barthes, La Chambre claire : note on the photograph, Paris : Cahiers du
cinéma/Gallimard/Seuil, 1980, p.93


Production : La Criée centre d'art contemporain, Rennes

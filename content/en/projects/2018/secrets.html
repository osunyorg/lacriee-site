silent film, 420 min











Secrets  is a seven-hour silent film that I shot on video in February 2017 in Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, and California, with the help of Bartolomé Sanson. Over the course of a month, we traveled more than 6,000 kilometers by car, passing giant cacti, rocks, and man-made lakes, as well as places steeped in utopian ideals—some of which had since been disillusioned (Arcosanti, Biosphere II…), constantly shifting between doubt and wonder, all against the backdrop of a complex and unsettling American political climate. Each shot is separated from the next by a blank frame; my poems unfold there, offering either an off-screen view or a close-up, depending on the moment. To film Secrets , I decided not to use a tripod, but rather to rest the camera on the surrounding objects: a car roof, the top of a cactus or a rock, or a shoulder. Although the shots are mostly static, they do move at times. The environment leaves its mark on the image. The landscape defies language; it reveals its strange stillness, changing only over millions of years, while the body filming it always seems ready to tremble, to be moved, to linger too long or not long enough; that body, for its part, will always be fleeting in comparison to the plant and mineral environment it encounters. This is the perspective of the passing traveler, who captures only a fragment of the landscape, always partial, superficial, and profound at the same time, and subjective, with his eyes, his memory. It is this composite landscape—both multifaceted and partial—that I want to share with the viewer: a flâneur’s landscape, made up of dozens of landscapes, based on the intuition that we are rarely in just one place at a time (my heart here, my feet there, my thoughts somewhere else entirely)… It is also a landscape that lies somewhere between a computer wallpaper, wallpaper, and a dog-eared postcard, with a few scattered words on the back to share with the one who “wasn’t there.”
